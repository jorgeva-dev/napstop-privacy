NapStop's core function is to ring at the exact moment you reach a point you marked, usually while you are asleep on a train or a bus. Without background location access the app cannot do that, and it has no purpose left.

NapStop collects location data to trigger your alarm even when the app is closed or not in use.

This access happens only while you have an active alarm that you created, and stops automatically once the alarm rings, is cancelled or is switched off.

All the logic runs on your own phone. NapStop uses the Geofencing API from Google Play Services, an Android system component, to detect entry into the area you defined. Your position is never transmitted to the developer or to any first-party server, because NapStop has no servers.

The alarms you create are stored in a local database inside the app, on your device.
